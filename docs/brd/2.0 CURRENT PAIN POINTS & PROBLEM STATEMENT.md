2.0 CURRENT PAIN POINTS & PROBLEM STATEMENT

2.1 Current State Overview

Living With Lolo currently manages client communications through a combination of individual Gmail inboxes, text messaging on personal mobile devices, and intermittent project documentation within Airtable. This approach results in inconsistent visibility into client interactions and limits the ability to track or audit message history across the organization.

Email communication is primarily handled through Gmail, without centralized routing or shared visibility. Text messaging lacks standardized workflows and record association, with no consistent tie between messages and project data. Internal team communication occurs via Google Chat (internal-only, not integrated with client-facing channels).

The result is a fragmented view of client correspondence, manual reconciliation between systems, and limited accountability for timely response handling.

2.2 Key Pain Points

2.2.1 Clients Don't Know Whom to Contact

2.2.1.1 LWL lacks a single generic email address or phone number for paid clients.

2.2.1.2 Clients must guess whether to contact the appropriate functional role (e.g., Design, Billing, Construction).

2.2.1.3 Externally, clients may use multiple personal accounts (husband, wife, work, personal); LWL must capture all of these contact details to maintain context.

2.2.1.4 This uncertainty creates redundant conversations, slower responses, and client frustration.

2.2.2 Fragmented Internal and External Communication

2.2.2.1 External: Clients reach out through a mix of Gmail threads, texts, and calls without knowing the correct point of contact.

2.2.2.2 Internal: Team members coordinate through Google Chat channels, Gmail threads, and occasional personal texts, making it difficult to trace prior decisions for a specific client or project.

2.2.2.3 There is no unified place to view the full client interaction history or link related internal notes.

2.2.3 Limited Visibility Across Projects

2.2.3.1 Neither leadership nor teammates can easily view a project's communication timeline without manually searching Gmail, texts, or chats.

2.2.3.2 Some inboxes are private, preventing authorized leaders or others from viewing previous exchanges.

2.2.3.3 There is no unified dashboard showing open inquiries or response status, leading to duplication and missed follow-ups.

2.2.4 No Designated Ownership or Escalation

2.2.4.1 Projects informally have a Point of Contact (POC), but this is not consistently recorded in any system. 

2.2.4.2 If the contacted POC is unavailable, there is no backup coverage or manual handoff process to ensure timely response. 

2.2.4.3 Response reliability depends on personal habits rather than consistent business rules.

2.2.5 No Central System for Communication Data

2.2.5.1 There is often nowhere to store conversation history other than individual inboxes.

2.2.5.2 Emails and texts live outside Airtable or QuickBooks, leaving projects disconnected from client context.

2.2.5.3 Without a hub, LWL cannot track response times, volume, or satisfaction trends.

2.2.6 Inconsistent Client Experience

2.2.6.1 Auto-replies, signatures, and tone vary by individual.

2.2.6.2 Some clients receive after-hours messages or multiple contacts from different team members.

2.2.6.3 This inconsistency undermines the calm, cohesive feel that defines LWL's brand.

2.3 Underlying Causes

2.3.1 Tool Fragmentation: Multiple unconnected platforms (Gmail, Google Chat, Google Voice, personal phones).

2.3.2 Process Inconsistency: Each department follows its own habits for logging or replying.

2.3.3 Lack of Automation: No routing, escalation, or standardized after-hours handling; manual forwarding is common.

2.3.4 Visibility Gaps: No real-time dashboards or searchable archives.

2.3.5 Cultural Dependence: Reliance on individual initiative rather than system-driven accountability.

2.4 Problem Statement

Living With Lolo’s current communication environment is disconnected and difficult to track, limiting the firm’s ability to deliver a seamless, high-touch client experience.

The absence of a unified communications hub leads to:

2.4.1 Lost or delayed messages when multiple channels (Gmail, texts, and chats) are used without central visibility.

2.4.2 Inconsistent tone and timing across team members, resulting in a less cohesive brand experience for clients.

2.4.3 Limited organizational visibility into client interactions and historical context for each project.

To protect its brand and support growth, LWL requires a single, centralized communications platform where:

2.4.4 Every client message (email, text, and internal note) is captured and linked to its project record in HubSpot Conversations.

2.4.5 Ownership is defined through a two-tier assignment structure—one Primary and one Backup Point of Contact (POC)—ensuring coverage through manual collaboration. 

2.4.6 The entire team can collaborate and respond within one shared interface that offers project-level visibility and relies on clear, human-driven procedures rather than complex automation.

This transformation will replace reactive and fragmented communication with a structured, human-driven system that supports clarity, accountability, and LWL’s signature white-glove service standard while maintaining simplicity in process and tooling.
